Participation Agreement, Assumption of Risk & Release of Liability

Please read this carefully. It is a legal document that affects your rights. By signing it, you give up the right to sue for certain injuries, including injuries caused by ordinary negligence.

This agreement is between you (“you” or “the Participant”) and [Business legal name], doing business as Mike Flynn Horsemanship, and Mike Flynn (together, “we” or “us”).

1. About the program

The Foundation Series is an online educational program. It runs for four weeks and includes video lessons, written materials, and feedback on videos you choose to send us. We are not present when you work with your horse. You decide whether, when, where and how to do each exercise, with your own horse or a horse you have permission to handle, at a location you choose. The Foundation Series is ground work only. It does not include riding.

2. Horses are dangerous. Injury can happen even when everyone is careful.

You understand that working with horses carries inherent risks that cannot be removed, however careful you are. These include, among others:

  • A horse may kick, bite, strike, rear, bolt, spook, pull back, step on you, knock you down, or crush you against a fence, wall or gate.
  • A horse's behaviour cannot always be predicted, even by experienced people, and even with a horse you know well.
  • Ground and weather conditions, equipment failure, fences, gates, other people and other animals can all cause or add to injury.
  • Injuries can be serious, including broken bones, head injury, permanent disability, or death.
  • Your horse may also be injured.

3. Your responsibilities

You agree that you are responsible for your own safety and your horse's. In particular, you agree to:

  • Use the safety checklist before every session: boots with a heel, gloves for rope work, a rope halter and suitable lead, a safe fenced area, someone who knows you are there, and a phone with you.
  • Never wrap a lead rope around your hand, arm or body.
  • Stop any exercise at any time you feel unsafe, and not continue past your own comfort.
  • Get in-person help from a qualified professional for any horse that kicks, bites, strikes, rears, bolts, or behaves dangerously.
  • Make sure your horse is healthy and sound enough for the exercises, and ask your veterinarian if you are unsure.
  • Get permission from the owner or manager of any property where you work your horse (for example, a boarding barn), and follow its rules.

4. Your health

You confirm that you are physically able to take part, or that you will only do the exercises you are able to do safely. If you have an injury, a medical condition, limited mobility or balance, or have been injured by a horse, we strongly recommend you talk to your doctor before starting. The ground-only and adapted versions of each lesson are general suggestions. They are not medical, physical therapy, or rehabilitation advice.

5. Limits of online feedback

Any feedback we give is based only on the video you send. We cannot see everything that happens off camera, and we cannot assess you, your horse, your equipment or your surroundings in person. Feedback is educational opinion. It is not a guarantee of any result or of your safety. You remain responsible for every decision you make with your horse.

6. Not professional advice

Nothing in the program is veterinary, medical, physical therapy, or legal advice.
