Who this is for

If you're still showing up, you're the right person for this.

Fear

"Being afraid doesn't make you wrong."

A horse is a 1,200-pound animal. Respecting that is healthy. The problem isn't fear. It's when fear makes the decisions instead of you. Fear shows you where the foundation isn't solid yet. That's information, not a stop sign.

Your body

"Your body doesn't disqualify you."

Injury, age, chronic pain. The clinics assume you're fully able. That's the industry failing to include you, not a verdict on whether you belong. Horsemanship is communication: consistency, clarity, presence. All three are open to you.

After an accident

"One bad day is not the story."

If a horse has hurt you, you don't push through it. You go back to where the conversation was still working and rebuild from there. Going back isn't failure. It's the most advanced thing you can do with a horse.

Riding, but…

"I ride, but something's missing."

He spooks. Won't stand at the block. Stiff on one side. Heavy on the rein. Those aren't riding problems. They're gaps from the ground, showing up in the saddle. Fix the foundation and the riding follows.

If it does not hold up over time, for horse and human, it is not horsemanship.

Mike Flynn

The approach

The standard stays high. The door stays open.

Most horse problems aren't caused by bad people. They're caused by people who were never taught what they needed to know. You didn't know what you didn't know. Now you're finding out.

This program will tell you the truth about your horse, and it will never make you feel small for asking. Each week you get short, plain lessons and one thing to practise with your horse. One thing, done right. Then put the horse away on a win.

  1. 01

    Foundation first

    Groundwork isn't a step toward riding. It is the conversation. What you build on the ground is exactly what you'll have everywhere else.

  2. 02

    Safety you can see

    Where to stand, what his ears and eyes are telling you, and when to stop. Taught before anything else, every time.

  3. 03

    Your pace, your body

    Every lesson has a ground-only path and an adapted version for limited strength, balance or mobility.

  4. 04

    Short, honest sessions

    Twenty minutes of honest work beats two hours of drilling. Foundation is built in honest sessions, not long ones.

The Foundation Series

Four weeks, all on the ground. Before you ask your horse for anything, you learn to read him and he learns he can trust you. You start together, as a small group, and Mike looks at a video of you and your horse every week.

  1. Week 1

    Read Before You Ask

    The first 60 seconds at the gate. Where's his attention? Where's his tension? Learn to read your horse before you do anything, and how to know when it's safe to start.

  2. Week 2

    Space Is the First Language

    Before you touch him, he's already read you. Your posture, your pace, your intent. Learn to ask for space calmly and clearly, without force.

  3. Week 3

    Pressure & Release

    The one principle under every technique. Ask lightly, release the moment he tries. The release is the reward. Most people hold on too long.

  4. Week 4

    The First Yield

    Moving the hindquarters off light pressure, calmly. It's the same first question in every discipline, from Grand Prix to the trail: can I trust the person holding the rope?

Every week includes

  • Short video lessons, filmed in real barns with real horses
  • One clear thing to practise, with a printable sheet
  • A ground-only and an adapted-mobility version
  • What to watch for: ears, eye, lick and chew, head drop

Mike's signature practice

Silent Sundays. Nothing verbal at all.

Every Sunday, you work your horse without a word. No whoa, no cluck, no kiss. When you're talking, your emotions come second. When you're quiet, you notice them, and once you notice them you can settle them. Your horse was picking them up the whole time. When you're settled on the inside, he settles with you.

Your horse doesn't listen to what you say. He listens to who you are when you say nothing.

Straight answers

Do I have to ride?

No. The Foundation Series is all ground work, and so are the first nine months if you keep going. The riding months each have a full ground-only path. Your horse doesn't need you to ride him to know you're his person.

Do I have to sign up for a whole year?

No. You start with the four-week Foundation Series. If it helps, you can carry on to the next series. If it doesn't, you've lost nothing but four weeks of honest work with your horse.

I ride regularly. Is this for me?

Yes. Spooking, not standing at the block, stiffness on one side, a horse that's heavy on the rein: those usually start on the ground. The Foundation Series fixes them there, so your riding follows.

I'm in my 50s or 60s and not very fit. Is this realistic?

Yes. Exercises are low-impact, and every lesson shows an adapted version. Adult learners bring patience, self-awareness and real motivation, which is a strong place to learn from.

How much time does it take?

About a 15–25 minute lesson each week, plus two or three short sessions with your horse. One thing, done right. Lessons stay available, so you can repeat a week whenever you need to.

My horse has hurt me. Is this safe for me?

Month 1 starts with reading your horse and knowing when to stop, which is exactly what you need when trust has been broken. You'll always work at the edge of your comfort, never past it. If you ever feel unsafe, stop and get in-person help.
